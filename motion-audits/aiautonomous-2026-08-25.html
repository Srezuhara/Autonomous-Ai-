A near-motionless app with two doors that slam

AppBuilder is a nine-agent code-generation pipeline: a marketing landing page in front, and five product screens behind it that a user watches for three to eight minutes at a stretch. The product screens have already had their motion stripped back hard — and it shows. What's left are two overlays that animate in and vanish out, and one hover transition that outruns its own budget.

What's off

The distribution is healthy — the mass sits in the responsive band exactly where a productivity tool wants it. Two things are misplaced. Dot 5 is a hover transition on a widget fixed to the corner of every product screen, so it fires incidentally, dozens of times a session, at 260ms — the one place the app's own sub-200ms rule is broken by something the user did not intend to trigger. Dots 1 and 2 are not slow; they do not exist. Both overlays enter with motion and leave by being deleted from the DOM.

Dot 6 at 420ms is a deliberate, documented exception — it encodes a value, and a progress bar that snaps between widths is unreadable. It is correct as written.

Important Emil Frequency rule

The always-present status widget expands on hover at 260ms

What.fstatus__detail transitions grid-template-rows from 0fr to 1fr over --duration-normal (260ms) on :hover. The widget is fixed to the corner of every product screen.

Why it mattersBecause it is fixed and always present, this fires incidentally — the pointer crosses it on the way somewhere else, dozens of times a session. That is the highest-frequency motion in the app, and it is the slowest interaction transition in it. Emil's frequency rule points the other way: the more often something fires, the less it should animate. It also breaks the project's own locked "sub-200ms or nothing" budget for product screens, and it animates a layout-triggering property, so each frame costs a reflow.

Recommended motionDrop to --duration-fast (150ms). Keep grid-template-rows — it is the correct technique for animating to an unknown auto height, and the reflow cost on one small fixed element is acceptable. Fade the inner content on the same 150ms so the text does not appear before it has room.

Issues to address
Critical Jakub Exit states

The nav drawer and its scrim leave by different rules

WhatThe scrim is conditionally rendered — {navOpen && <div className="nav-scrim" />}. It fades in over 150ms via navScrimIn. On close it is removed from the DOM instantly. The drawer beside it is not conditionally rendered, so its transform transition still runs: the panel slides out over 200ms.

Why it mattersThese are two halves of one gesture and they come apart on the way out. The dark overlay blinks off, revealing the page at full brightness while the navigation panel is still visibly sliding across it. The open is choreographed; the close looks like a rendering bug. This is the app's primary navigation on every screen below 900px.

Recommended motionKeep the scrim mounted and drive its opacity from a class, so both halves are CSS transitions that leave together — 150ms for the scrim fade against the drawer's 200ms slide, so the overlay clears just before the panel lands. Transitions, not keyframes, specifically because the drawer can be re-opened mid-close and must retarget rather than queue.

Critical Jakub Exit states

The rebuild dialog has an entrance and no exit at all

WhatRebuildModal returns null when closed. The backdrop fades in over 150ms and the dialog translates 4px up into place; on close, both are deleted from the DOM in the same frame. The blurred backdrop covering the whole viewport disappears instantly.

Why it mattersA full-viewport blurred overlay vanishing between two frames is the most abrupt state change in the product, and it happens on a confirmation flow — the moment the user most needs to feel that their choice registered. The component was otherwise rebuilt to a high standard this cycle (focus trap, Escape, restoration, role="dialog"); the missing exit is the one thing left that makes it feel unfinished.

Recommended motionExit subtler than the enter, per Jakub's asymmetry rule: opacity only, ~120ms, no translate — the dialog recedes rather than retracing its entrance backwards. Framer Motion is already a dependency (currently confined to the landing); AnimatePresence around the modal is the smallest correct change, and scaleIn in lib/motion.ts already has the right exit variant defined and unused.

Issues to address
Important Jhey Jakub

The headline is the last thing on the page to become readable

WhatThe hero runs a delay ramp — 0.06 / 0.13 / 0.20 / 0.27s — across the h1, the lede, the CTA row and the stat list, each playing fadeUp at 420ms with a 24px throw and a 6px blur.

Why it mattersThe h1 is the page's primary message and it is deliberately blurred for the first fifth of a second, fully legible only around 480ms. The cascade is the right idea — it just starts on the wrong element. Everything below the headline benefits from arriving in sequence; the headline itself gains nothing from waiting, and the page reads as slow to load rather than as considered.

Recommended motionTake the h1 out of the ramp: zero delay, no blur, a short 6px rise on the app's out curve. Keep the existing glide and blur for everything beneath it, shifting each up one slot. The cascade still reads as a cascade — it now flows from the headline instead of arriving after it.

Jakub Krehel — Production Polish

Emil was the primary lens and found almost nothing, because his rules had already been applied deliberately across the product screens. The live problems were all exit states, which is Jakub's territory — an app that animates in and deletes out is polished on the way in and unfinished on the way out.

To lean more on Emil — audit the 260ms and 420ms transitions against real interaction counts rather than against the token names.
To lean more on Jakub — treat every conditional render in the product as an exit-state question, not just the two overlays flagged here.
To lean more on Jhey — the empty and error states are the only product surfaces with room to experiment without breaking the restraint contract.
